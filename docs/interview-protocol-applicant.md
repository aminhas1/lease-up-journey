# Interview protocol: a household that went through the lottery

**For:** one applicant who went from Housing Connect application to move-in (or to a final decision). **Length:** 30 to 40 minutes. **Format:** video or phone, recorded only with consent, notes in the template at the end.

## What this interview is for

The research so far says applicants are lost to timing, notification and clarity more than to eligibility: a 10-weekday window they didn't see, an upload arrow that only appears when selected, a portal that locks after one submission, no receipt, deposits that have to be explained line by line, and income math they first meet in the rejection letter. All of that comes from forum posts, press and Council testimony. None of it comes from someone I talked to. This interview is to hear one full story in order, find where it confirms or breaks those claims, and collect the artifacts the research couldn't: what the request notice actually said, how the submission really happened, and what came back.

Three things I most want to learn:

1. What the document request looked like when it arrived, and how she understood it.
2. What she did physically to gather and submit, and what went wrong or almost went wrong.
3. What happened after she pressed submit, and how she knew.

## Ground rules to say out loud

- I'm learning how this works. I'm not with the city, a developer or a marketing agent, and nothing she says affects any application.
- No need to share any document, number or account. If she wants to describe a letter or screen from memory, that's plenty. If she offers a screenshot or a redacted notice, I'll take it.
- I'll ask "what happened next" a lot. Rambling is good. Skipping a step is fine.
- Ask to record; if no, say I'll type notes.

## The conversation

Times are a guide. Follow the story, not the clock, but keep 8 minutes for the last two sections.

### 1. Her situation at the time (3 min)

- When did you apply, and when did you move in? Which building, roughly, and what kind of unit?
- Who was in the household on the application? Who was working, and how were they paid (salary, hourly, weekly, cash, self-employed, benefits)?
- Did you have a voucher or any subsidy?
- Had you applied to lotteries before this one? How many, roughly?

*Listen for:* pay frequency (weekly means 4 stubs, not 2), self-employment, benefits (the award-letter safe harbor), vouchers (contacted at every income tier), and whether this was her tenth lottery or her first.

### 2. The moment the number came up (7 min)

- How did you find out your number was reached? Email, text, a letter, logging in on your own?
- What did the request say? Can you remember the list? How long did it give you?
- What did you understand you had to do, and was there anything on the list you didn't understand or didn't think applied to you?
- Was it in the language you'd chosen? Did it feel real, or did you wonder if it was a scam?
- Did it say how to send things: in Housing Connect, by email, in person, through another website?

*Probes:* "What did you do in the first hour after you saw it?" "Was there a deadline date on it, or a number of days?" "Did anyone else see it before you did, like a family member?"

*Listen for:* whether she saw the notice at all before the clock started, whether the list matched her household, a second portal (Pronto, DocuSign, a company name), the words "action needed".

### 3. Gathering (8 min)

- Walk me through getting each thing. Which were already in your hands? Which did you have to ask someone for?
- Which one took longest? What did you have to do to get it?
- Did you have to write anything yourself, like a letter explaining something, a statement that you had no income from somewhere, or an explanation of money in your bank account?
- Did anyone help you: a family member, a counselor, a community organization, an employer?
- Did anything on the list not exist for you? What did you do about it?

*Probes:* "Did they ask about deposits in your bank statements?" "Did they ask for anything that wasn't on the original list?" "Were any of your documents too old by the time they looked at them?"

*Listen for:* the deposit explanation (how she formatted it, whether she knew the rule), items added after the first list, the employer who wouldn't respond, documents aging past 120 days, a counselor's role.

### 4. Submitting (7 min)

- How did you actually send them? On a phone or a computer? Photos or scans or PDFs?
- Show me, or describe, the screen. Where was the button? Did you find it on the first try?
- Did you send everything at once or in pieces? Did anything stop you from adding something later?
- How did you know they got it?
- Did you send the same things more than once, or by more than one route? Why?

*Probes:* "Did the website ever time out, refuse a file, or lose something?" "Did you also email them?" "Did you keep your own copy of what you sent?"

*Listen for:* the hidden upload arrow, the one-shot lock, the missing receipt, email as the paper trail, file types that failed, uploading to the general documents area by mistake.

### 5. What came back (7 min)

- What was the next thing you heard from them, and how long after?
- Did they ask for anything more? What, and how much time did they give you?
- Was anything rejected or sent back? What reason did they give, and did you understand it?
- Was there an interview or appointment? What was it like?
- Did you ever have to appeal? What did you write, and what happened?
- From submitting to hearing "approved", how long was it? What did you do in between?

*Probes:* "When they explained the income number, did it match what you expected?" "Did anyone ever tell you the application was rejected by mistake?" "Did you call anyone, and did anyone pick up?"

*Listen for:* a second request, the three-figure income calculation (W-2, stub average, year-to-date), bonuses or overtime, automated "window expired" messages, months of silence, calling HPD.

### 6. Looking back (5 min)

- If a friend's number came up tomorrow, what would you tell them to have ready, and what would you tell them to watch out for?
- What was the single most confusing moment?
- What was the single most stressful moment, and was it the same one?
- If you could change one thing about how the documents part works, what would it be?
- Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?

### 7. Close (2 min)

- Thank her. Ask whether she'd be willing to look at a rough mockup of a document-request screen next week for ten minutes.
- Ask whether she knows anyone else who went through it, including someone who was rejected or gave up. A rejected applicant is the interview I most need next.
- Ask again, gently, whether she has the original request notice or rejection letter she'd be comfortable sharing with personal details covered.

## Things not to do

- Don't explain the handbook to her, even when she gets a rule wrong. The gap between what she believed and what the rule says is the finding.
- Don't ask leading questions like "was the upload button hard to find?" Ask "where was the button?" and let her describe it.
- Don't ask her income, her rent, or her log number. If she volunteers them, don't write them down.
- Don't show a prototype in this interview. This one is for the story.
